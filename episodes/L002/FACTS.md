# L002 fact sheet: every factual claim in the narration and its source

These are mainstream sources (peer-reviewed papers, excavation reports and standard references). Where the evidence is debated, the narration says so, and the screen shows a yellow tag ("STILL DEBATED", "probably", "by one estimate"). Light-hearted lines about Fardin (hunger, hitch-hiking, "loves every second") are character comedy, not historical claims.

| Claim in narration | Source / basis |
|---|---|
| Built for pharaoh Khufu, Fourth Dynasty, about 4,500 years ago (c. 2560 BC) | Standard Egyptian chronology; e.g. Lehner, *The Complete Pyramids* (1997); Britannica "Pyramids of Giza" |
| Giza is on the west bank of the Nile | Geography; Britannica |
| Core blocks were quarried on the Giza plateau itself; those quarries are still visible next to the pyramids | Lehner & Hawass, *Giza and the Pyramids* (2017): Khufu's main quarry lies south of the pyramid |
| Fine white casing limestone came from Tura, across the river (east bank) | Lehner (1997); Klemm & Klemm, *The Stones of the Pyramids* (2010) |
| Tools: copper chisels, wooden mallets, wooden levers, no iron tools; copper needed constant re-sharpening | Stocks, *Experiments in Egyptian Archaeology* (2003); Arnold, *Building in Egypt* (1991) |
| Channels cut around blocks, which were then levered free | Arnold (1991), quarrying methods |
| Average block weighs about 2.5 tons | Commonly cited average (Britannica; Lehner 1997) |
| About 2.3 million blocks, presented as an estimate ("by one estimate") | Widely cited estimate (Britannica; Lehner 1997) |
| The Nile flooded every year, and boats got closer to Giza during the flood | Annual inundation; Giza harbour and canal basins: Lehner & Tallet; Tallet, *The Red Sea Scrolls* (2021) |
| Diary of Merer: papyri found in 2013 at Wadi al-Jarf on the Red Sea coast; the oldest known inscribed papyri | Tallet & Marouard (2014), *Near Eastern Archaeology* 77(1); Tallet, *Les papyrus de la mer Rouge I* (2017) |
| Merer was an inspector; his logbook records moving Tura limestone to Giza by boat | Tallet (2017, 2021) |
| His crew was about 40 men | Tallet (2021): a "phyle" of about 40 men under Merer |
| Papyri date to the final years of Khufu's reign | Tallet (2017): dated to around Khufu's 27th year |
| Blocks were dragged on wooden sledges by teams with ropes | Arnold (1991); tomb depictions; sledge remains |
| A pile of sand builds up in front of a sledge on dry sand, while wet sand is stiffer | Fall et al. (2014), *Physical Review Letters* 112, 175502 |
| The right amount of water cuts the pulling force by about half, and too much water makes it harder again | Fall et al. (2014), University of Amsterdam press release, 29 April 2014 |
| Egyptians painted the water trick in a tomb centuries later (narrator says "centuries later") | Tomb of Djehutihotep, el-Bersheh, Middle Kingdom (c. 1900 BC) |
| Workers' town south of the plateau: Heit el-Ghurab, the "Lost City of the Pyramid Builders" | Lehner & AERA excavations (from 1988); Lehner & Wetterstrom, *Giza Reports* vol. 1 (2007) |
| Bakeries with stacks of clay bread moulds were found there | Lehner (AERA), bakeries excavated in 1991 |
| Bread and beer were Egypt's everyday staples (given as general Egyptian diet) | Standard Egyptology (e.g. Samuel 1996 on bread and beer) |
| Large numbers of cattle, sheep and goat bones show workers ate meat | Redding, faunal analysis at Heit el-Ghurab (AERA) |
| "Some researchers think" the animals came from Delta farms | Redding (2010s) estimates of Delta cattle estates supplying Giza, presented as a hypothesis |
| Work-gang names were painted inside the pyramid, e.g. "Friends of Khufu" | Graffiti in the relieving chambers above the King's Chamber; Lehner (1997), Lehner & Hawass (2017) |
| Workers' skeletons show healed broken bones, evidence of medical care | Hawass, workers' cemetery excavations (from 1990); reported in Lehner & Hawass (2017) |
| Workers buried in their own tombs beside the pyramids; most archaeologists see this as evidence against slavery | Hawass (2010, press statements); Lehner; the narration attributes this to "most archaeologists" |
| Many were "probably" farmers, in rotating crews, paid in food (rations) | Lehner's rotating-labour model; rations of bread, beer and meat |
| Workforce given only as "thousands" | Estimates vary (often 10,000 to 30,000), so no single figure is claimed |
| Construction took about 20 years (traditional estimate) | Herodotus, *Histories* 2.124, and the standard estimate in modern references |
| How blocks were raised is still debated: straight, spiral or internal ramp | Lehner (1997); Houdin's internal-ramp hypothesis (2006); the narration says "Scholars still debate it" |
| A straight ramp to the top would have had to be enormous | Standard argument against single straight-ramp models (Arnold 1991) |
| Hatnub quarry ramp found in 2018, with a staircase on each side and post holes, dated to Khufu's reign | IFAO and University of Liverpool (Gourdon, Hatnub mission), announced October 2018; Khufu inscriptions at the site |
| Casing of polished white Tura limestone, cut at an angle to form smooth faces | Lehner (1997); surviving casing stones at the base |
| Original height about 146 m (481 ft) | Petrie (1883); Britannica (146.6 m) |
| Base covers more than 5 hectares (about 13 acres, roughly 7 football fields) | Base about 230.3 m square = 5.3 ha; a football pitch is about 0.7 ha |
| Tallest human-made structure for nearly 4,000 years, until Lincoln Cathedral (c. AD 1311) | Britannica; standard reference: Lincoln Cathedral's spire surpassed it around 1311 |
| Most of the casing was later removed for building stone | Removed largely after the 1303 earthquake for use in Cairo (Britannica; Lehner 1997) |
| Cleopatra lived closer in time to the Moon landing than to the pyramid's construction | Arithmetic: Cleopatra died in 30 BC, about 2,530 years after c. 2560 BC and about 2,000 years before 1969 |
| Pompeii teaser: AD 79, Vesuvius erupted | Standard date (Pliny the Younger) |

Not claimed (deliberately avoided): any exact workforce number, exact ramp design, gilded capstone, slaves or aliens (shown only as the myth that is debunked), and the Djehutihotep painting being from Khufu's time (the narration says "centuries later").
