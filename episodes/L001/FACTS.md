# L001 fact sheet — every claim in the narration, with its basis

All claims are mainstream and hedged where the science is uncertain (the narrator says so on screen with a yellow tag).

| Claim in narration | Basis |
|---|---|
| Jebel Irhoud (Morocco) fossils ~300,000 years old, announced 2017; oldest known *Homo sapiens* fossils | Hublin et al. 2017 & Richter et al. 2017, *Nature* (≈315 ± 34 ka) |
| Faces similar to ours; braincase longer and lower; brain size within modern range, rounder (globular) shape appeared later | Hublin et al. 2017; Neubauer et al. 2018, *Science Advances* |
| Burnt flint tools were used to date the site | Richter et al. 2017 (thermoluminescence on heated flints, ~315 ka) |
| At times North Africa was much greener (grasslands, lakes, rivers) | "Green Sahara" humid periods recur through the Pleistocene; hedged "at times" |
| Gazelles were the most common animal bones; many bones carry stone-tool cut marks | Hublin et al. 2017 faunal analysis |
| Gazelle top speed up to ~80 km/h; humans ~45 km/h | Thomson's gazelle ~80–90 km/h; Usain Bolt peak ≈44.7 km/h |
| Some stone points look made for hafting; throwing vs thrusting is debated | Middle Stone Age Levallois points; hafting & spear use debated (tagged "still debating") |
| Marrow cracking > 1 million years | Percussion-marked bones from >2 million years ago (e.g. Kanjera South, Gona) |
| How often they cooked is uncertain; plants rarely preserve | Hedged in narration |
| Levallois (prepared-core) technique; used for >200,000 years across Africa and beyond | Emerges ~300–400 ka, common in Middle Stone Age / Middle Palaeolithic until ~40 ka |
| Fire use shown by burnt flint and bone | Jebel Irhoud heated lithics; habitual fire use widespread by this period (narrator: "maybe cooked food") |
| Olorgesailie, Kenya: obsidian carried up to ~90 km; red and black pigments; trade debated | Brooks et al. 2018 & Potts et al. 2018, *Science* (~320 ka, sources 25–95 km away) |
| Lions, leopards and spotted hyenas shared the land with early humans in Africa | Pleistocene African fossil record |
| Fire *probably* helped keep predators away | Hedged ("probably", tag "likely, but hard to prove") |
| Border Cave, South Africa: grass bedding ~200,000 years old, laid on ash, possibly to deter insects | Wadley et al. 2020, *Science* |
| Neanderthal ancestors in Europe | e.g. Sima de los Huesos (~430 ka) early Neanderthal lineage |
| Denisovans in Asia, known mostly from DNA | Denisova Cave, Baishiya Karst Cave (hedged: lineage present, fossils rare) |
| *Homo naledi*, South Africa | Rising Star, dated 236–335 ka (Dirks et al. 2017) |
| Archaic human in Zambia | Kabwe (Broken Hill) skull dated ~299 ka (Grün et al. 2020) |
| Probably some of the last *Homo erectus* on Java | Javan *H. erectus* persisted until ~117–108 ka (Ngandong, Rizal et al. 2020) — "probably" |
| At least five kinds of humans *may* have shared the planet | Count of the above lineages; hedged with "may" |
| Much later, interbreeding; many people today carry a little Neanderthal or Denisovan DNA (~1–2% Neanderthal in non-Africans) | Green et al. 2010; Prüfer et al. 2014; Reich et al. 2010 |
| "24-hour day" comparison: farming ≈ 11:02 PM, pyramids ≈ 11:38 PM | Arithmetic: 12,000/300,000 × 24 h = 57.6 min before midnight; 4,500/300,000 × 24 h = 21.6 min |
| Clothing / language | Not claimed (unknown) — characters wear simple hide wraps as a stylistic choice |
