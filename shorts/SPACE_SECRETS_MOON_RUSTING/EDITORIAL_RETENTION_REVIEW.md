# Editorial & retention review — "The Moon Is Rusting"
No virality score is claimed. This is a qualitative review of the actual render. Post-publication retention data (/yt-retention) will be the real test.

## Opening (0–3 s), Stage A decision
- Three openings were rendered (90 frames each) and inspected at frames 0/10/20/30/40/50/59/75.
- **A (macro → whip pullback → Moon over Earth + O⁺ stream): chosen.** It is the only variant that shows the rust and the Earth connection within 1.2 s, and its last frame is literally "And Earth may be why."
- **B (push-in from the wide shot):** spent 1.3 s on a slow approach and ended on grains with no Earth.
- **C (Earth over the lunar horizon):** strong frame 0, but Earth was ~10× oversized, which breaks the accurate-geometry rule.
- **Muted-phone test:** frame 0 shows red-flecked grey grains under THE MOON IS RUSTING?!; by 1.0 s the Moon hangs over Earth, and by 2 s a glowing stream links them. It reads without sound.
- **Hook text score:** the mandated line scores 38 (WEAK) on hookscore.py, weakest on STAKES. It was kept verbatim, as the brief requires.

## Narrative chain
IMPOSSIBLE DISCOVERY (0–11 s) → SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE (11–27 s: M3, poles, near side) → POSSIBLE EXPLANATION (27–48 s: O⁺ escape, magnetotail, Moon in the tail, conceptual oxidation) → NEW EVIDENCE / BIGGER MYSTERY (48–68 s: Chang'e-6 crystals, far side, impacts) → SYNTHESIS (68–78 s: two processes) → PAYOFF (78–94 s).

## New visual revelation every 6–10 s (measured from SCENE_TIMELINE.csv)
- 22 shots over 94 s, about 4.3 s each. The longest single shot is the "callback" (84–91 s, ~6 s), followed by the wide payoff.
- No two consecutive shots share a composition. Macro, low-angle terrain, black-sky horizon, orbital, nadir scan, polar orbit, comparison, Earth limb, magnetosphere wide, reverse angle, micrometre crystal, lander, impact, split screen, telephoto and the wide system shot all appear.

## Cinematic vs diagram share
- **Diagram-led shots:** the nadir scan (2.5 s), the near/far comparison (~3.6 s) and the magnetosphere wide (4.3 s), about 10.4 s, or ~11% of the runtime.
- **The rest (~89%)** is terrain, globe, macro, space-environment or reconstruction imagery. That meets the ≥70% target.

## What works
- **Hook:** the hook resolves its own question visually: rust at frame 0, then Earth, then the stream.
- **Ending:** the ending calls back to the opening image (Moon over Earth with the stream), now understood. It then pulls out to a wide Earth–Moon shot with the title, which ties the last frame to the first.
- **Honest scale:** scale is handled honestly. Earth appears at its true apparent size for each telephoto lens, and the final wide shot is labelled "sizes to scale, distance compressed".
- **Scientific hedging:** it is carried on screen (hypothesis, illustrative, CGI concept), so the narration never needs a disclaimer line.

## Retention risks (honest)
1. **No authentic mission imagery** (Chandrayaan-1, Chang'e-6, or the actual SEM micrographs). Network access to NASA, ISRO, CNSA and journal media is blocked here, so these are labelled reconstructions. The spacecraft and lander models are simplified and the weakest visuals in the film. **Recommended:** before posting, swap in licensed real imagery if available (NASA/ISRO M3 maps, the CNSA Chang'e-6 sample photo), or keep the reconstructions as labelled.
2. **Procedural terrain** holds up in motion but is softer than true photogrammetry, especially in the telephoto panorama (74–78 s).
3. **The hook line scores WEAK** on stakes. A stakes-forward variant ("The Moon is rusting — and the oxygen may come from your planet's sky") would likely score higher. It was not used because the hook is mandated.
4. **The 12 s from 27–42 s** is dense physics. It is broken into three distinct angles, but it is still the most demanding stretch for casual viewers.
