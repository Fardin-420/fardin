# Science sources — "This Planet Is Darker Than Coal" (TrES-2b)
I checked the claims against the paper's arXiv abstract and the 2011 CfA press-release coverage listed below. The journal site was not reachable from this build machine, so I used indexed copies.

## 1. Original paper (2011)
**Kipping, D. M. & Spiegel, D. S. 2011, "Detection of visible light from the darkest world", *MNRAS Letters* 417, L88–L92** (arXiv:1108.2297).

What it supports:
- **The detection:** Kepler photometry shows TrES-2b's orbital phase variation at 3.7σ. The amplitude is **6.5 ± 1.9 parts per million**, the lowest-amplitude phase variation found at the time. The video says "a few parts per million" and shows "~6.5 parts per million" on screen.
- **Why "< 1%":**
  - If the whole signal were reflected light, the geometric albedo would be 0.0253 ± 0.0072.
  - The authors' models say a large part of the signal is **thermal emission**, so the true albedo is lower still: **under 1%**.
  - That is why the video separates reflected light from emitted light. It doesn't treat the measured signal as all reflection.
- **What the measurement was:** Kepler did not photograph the planet or its darkness. It measured tiny changes in the combined light of the star and planet. The video says this and labels the light curve as a schematic.

## 2. Press release and quotes (August 2011)
Source: Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics, "Alien world is blacker than coal" (EurekAlert / ScienceDaily, August 11, 2011).

| Claim | What the coverage says |
|---|---|
| Comparison | Less than 1% reflected makes it blacker than coal, and darker than any planet or moon in our solar system |
| Paint | Kipping: "considerably less reflective than black acrylic paint" |
| Host star and distance | GSC 03549-02811 in Draco, about **750 light-years** away. The brief said ~700; the video uses the CfA figure. |
| Red glow | Spiegel: the planet "emits a faint red glow, much like a burning ember or the coils on an electric stove", because it is over 1,000 °C (~1,800 °F) |
| Absorbers | Gaseous sodium, potassium and titanium oxide were proposed, but none fully explains the darkness. The video presents them as suspects and says the cause is still a mystery. |

- **Wording caveat:** my copy of the CfA release text was truncated, so some quotes come from secondary coverage. Their wording may differ slightly from the official release.

## 3. Background
| Fact | Value used in the video |
|---|---|
| Name and discovery | TrES-2b = Kepler-1b, a transiting hot Jupiter found by the TrES survey in 2006 |
| Orbit | About 2.47 days ("every two and a half days") |
| Comparison albedos | Approximate geometric albedos: Jupiter ~0.54, Earth ~0.43, Moon ~0.12 (rounded textbook values) |

## Constraints from the brief
- **Not invisible, not a black hole:** never called invisible or a black hole. The black paint and coal comparisons concern reflectivity only.
- **Not rocky:** shown as a banded gas giant with no surface.
- **Not a photograph:** every appearance shot is labelled CGI or an artist's impression. In some shots the planet is brightened slightly so its banding shows; this is noted on screen ("brightness exaggerated").
- **Reflected vs thermal:** the narration says "That glow isn't reflected starlight. It's heat."
