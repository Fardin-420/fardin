CLAIM -> BASIS
- The Moon has orbited Earth for ~4.5 billion years. Lunar samples / giant-impact formation ~4.4–4.5 Gyr ago (NASA Moon facts).
- The Moon is the dominant tide raiser; the Sun's tidal force is ~46 % of the Moon's. Tidal force ∝ M/d³;
  NOAA Tides tutorial ("the Sun's tide-generating force is about half that of the Moon").
- Spring and neap tides come from Sun–Moon alignment. NOAA Tides tutorial.
- Real tides rotate around amphidromic points shaped by basins. NOAA Tides tutorial; standard oceanography.
- Full-Moon ground illumination ~0.1–0.3 lux; starlight/airglow ~0.001 lux. Standard photometric values
  (e.g. Kyba et al. 2017, "Red is the new black: how the colour of urban skyglow varies with cloud cover").
- Coral mass spawning is cued partly by moonlight / lunar cycle. Babcock et al. 1986 (GBR); Lin et al. 2021, PNAS,
  "Moonlight and darkness cues for spawning".
- Predator–prey activity changes with moonlight (lunar phobia/philia). Prugh & Golden 2014, J. Anim. Ecol. (meta-analysis).
- Sea-turtle hatchlings orient toward the brighter, lower seaward horizon. Salmon et al. 1992; Witherington & Martin
  (FWRI Tech. Rep. TR-2).
- Tidal friction slows Earth's rotation and moves the Moon outward (angular-momentum transfer). NASA Moon facts; standard
  celestial mechanics.
- Day length in the past: ~21.9 h about 620 Myr ago (tidal rhythmites, Williams 2000, Rev. Geophys.); ~400 days/yr in the
  Devonian (Wells 1963, Nature, coral growth bands). On screen they're labelled "approximate".
- Lunar recession ~3.8 cm/yr, measured by Lunar Laser Ranging to Apollo retro-reflectors. NASA; Williams & Boggs 2016.
- Earth's axial tilt ≈ 23.44° today; it varies between ~22.1° and 24.5° over a ~41,000-year cycle. Laskar et al. 2004 (A&A).
- Without the Moon, SOME models give large chaotic obliquity swings (0°–85°): Laskar, Joutel & Robutel 1993, Nature 361.
  OTHER models find more limited variation: Lissauer, Barnes & Chambers 2012, Icarus 217. The film presents this as an
  open question, not a prediction.
- Annual-mean sunlight vs latitude (insolation graph): computed in the film from orbital geometry (solar constant 1361
  W/m², daily-mean insolation integrated over a year).
- Sun ≈ 400× the Moon's diameter and ≈ 390–400× farther: Sun 1.39 million km across, ~150 million km away; Moon 3,474 km,
  ~384,400 km (NASA Sun / Moon facts).
- Total tidal dissipation ≈ 3.7 TW, ~1 TW of it in the deep ocean: Egbert & Ray 2000, Nature 405.
- 12 people walked on the Moon, Apollo 11–17 (1969–1972). NASA.
- 66 million years × 3.8 cm/yr ≈ 2,500 km. Labelled "at today's rate"; the rate has varied (Bills & Ray 1999, GRL).

WHAT THE FILM DOESN'T CLAIM (per the brief)
- No instant worldwide tsunamis. Earth's rotation doesn't stop. Solar tides remain. Earth keeps orbiting the Sun.
- Axial instability is shown as a long-term scientific uncertainty, never a guaranteed catastrophe.
