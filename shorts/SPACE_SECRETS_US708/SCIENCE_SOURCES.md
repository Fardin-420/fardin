# Science sources: "This Star Is Escaping the Milky Way" (US 708)
I checked every claim against the sources below (the paper's arXiv record, the W. M. Keck Observatory and University of Hawaiʻi releases, and the later papers named here). The journal sites themselves are blocked on this build machine's network, so I used indexed copies of them.

## 1. The speed, the origin and the supernova idea (2015)
**Paper:** Geier, S. et al. 2015, "The fastest unbound star in our Galaxy ejected by a thermonuclear supernova", *Science* 347, 1126–1128, doi:10.1126/science.1259063 (arXiv:1503.01650).
**Press releases:** W. M. Keck Observatory, "Thermonuclear Supernova Ejects Galaxy's Fastest Star"; University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa.

What it supports:
- **Speed:** about 1,200 km/s in the Galactic rest frame, which makes US 708 unbound from the Milky Way. 1,200 km/s ≈ 2.68 million mph, which the video rounds to "roughly 2.7 million".
- **Not the black hole:** tracing its path back makes the Galactic Center very unlikely as the origin, which rules out the supermassive black hole. The path instead points back to the Galactic disk.
- **What the star is:** a compact, helium-rich hot subdwarf (sdO/B) that rotates unusually fast. That spin is consistent with a past interaction with a close companion.
- **Proposed origin:** US 708 was once in an ultracompact binary, transferring helium to a massive white dwarf. The white dwarf exploded as a thermonuclear (Type Ia) supernova, and the surviving star was released at about its orbital speed. An orbit only minutes long is needed to reach this speed.
- **Timing:** ejected about 14 million years ago (the authors' estimate). The video says "millions of years ago" and shows "~14 million years ago (estimate)".

## 2. The case isn't closed
- **Liu, Z.-W. et al. 2021,** *A&A* (arXiv:2109.09980): a helium-star donor massive enough to match US 708's other properties has trouble getting close enough to reach its high speed. This supports "some models struggle".
- **Later speed estimates:** later work cites speeds of roughly 900–1,000 km/s. The video gives these as "later estimates ~900–1,000 km/s" and still says the star seems fast enough to escape.
  - **Not independently checked:** I couldn't open the paper that revises the speed with Gaia data. The 900–1,000 km/s range comes from a 2024 paper that cites it. Check it before quoting an exact figure.

## 4. Rounded or illustrative values
- **Surface temperature:** "~45,000 °C (est.)" on the close-up is a rounded, illustrative value for a hot sdO-type star. It isn't a quoted measurement of US 708 and isn't spoken.
- **Masses and sizes:** the binary uses rough masses (0.35 and 1.0 Msun). The white dwarf is drawn small and the helium star near its Roche limit, roughly in proportion; the reconstruction is labelled.
