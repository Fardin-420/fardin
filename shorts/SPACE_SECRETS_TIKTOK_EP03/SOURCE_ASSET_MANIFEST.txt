RESEARCH REFERENCES (supplied by the client; contents used as stated below)
1. NASA — "NASA's Hubble Finds a True Blue Planet" (2013):
   https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-finds-a-true-blue-planet/
   -> deep-blue colour measured by Hubble (STIS); ~63 light-years; dayside near 2,000 °F; the colour may come from a hazy
      atmosphere with silicate particles, which could condense into glass droplets (a proposed possibility).
2. NASA — "5,400mph Winds Discovered Hurtling Around Planet Outside Solar System" (2015):
   https://science.nasa.gov/universe/exoplanets/5400mph-winds-discovered-hurtling-around-planet-outside-solar-system/
   -> ~5,400 mph (~2 km/s) winds measured (Univ. of Warwick, HARPS spectrograph, ground telescope); ~7× the speed of sound.
3. NASA — "First Map of an Exoplanet Atmosphere" (Spitzer, 2007):
   https://science.nasa.gov/resource/first-map-of-an-exoplanet-atmosphere/
   -> first temperature map of an exoplanet; hottest region offset from the point facing the star (used for the
      night-side glow and the shifted hot spot in the heat scene).
NOTE: science.nasa.gov and all NASA/ESA image hosts were blocked by this environment's network policy, so the pages
couldn't be re-opened while editing. The figures above match the client's script and those references.
Other numbers on screen (standard values): orbit ~2.2 days; ~0.031 AU from its star (~13× closer than Mercury is to the
Sun, 0.39 AU); radius ~1.1 Jupiter (~12–13 Earths, Earth drawn to scale); constellation Vulpecula; Earth's fastest
measured winds ≈ 300 mph (1999 Bridge Creek–Moore tornado, Doppler radar).
Distance: NASA's article gives ~63 ly; newer Gaia parallax gives ~64.5 ly. The script's "about sixty-three" is kept.

VISUALS (all original, rendered frame by frame)
- HD 189733 b: procedural gas-giant renderer (planet.py). Zonal bands with differential flow, Rayleigh-blue limb, soft
  terminator, night-side thermal glow, eastward-shifted hot spot. An artistic, scientifically informed visualization,
  NOT an image of the planet (no image of it exists).
- Host star HD 189733 A (orange K dwarf): procedural star (star.py).
- Earth (scale inset): Solar System Scope textures, CC BY 4.0, based on NASA imagery (via the three.js repository). Credited on screen.
- Glass shards, cracks, silicate haze, condensing droplets, superstorm, sideways glass rain, cloud dive, lightning,
  thermometer, orbit, distance scale, observation cards: original particle and volumetric simulations and motion graphics in code.
- No NASA/ESA imagery, stock footage, AI-generated images, or YouTube/TikTok clips are used.
- Labels on screen: "SCIENTIFIC VISUALIZATION · not real footage" throughout; "NOT RECORDED FOOTAGE" and "NEVER SEEN" on the
  glass-rain sequence; "a possibility scientists propose · not observed" on the droplets.
