CLAIM — STATUS — BASIS
- Enceladus is a small icy moon of Saturn, ~504 km across (about Arizona's width) — MEASURED — NASA Enceladus facts.
- Plumes of water vapour and ice grains erupt from four south-polar "tiger stripe" fractures (~130 km long, ~35 km apart)
  — MEASURED (Cassini images and in-situ sampling) — Porco et al. 2006, Science 311; Spencer et al. 2006.
- Cassini flew through the plume several times (2008–2015); the deepest pass (E21, Oct 2015) was ~49 km above the surface
  — MEASURED — NASA/JPL Cassini mission pages.
- A global subsurface ocean of liquid water lies beneath the ice — STRONG INFERENCE from libration (wobble) measurements
  — Thomas et al. 2016, Icarus 264; gravity: Iess et al. 2014, Science 344 (regional sea first).
- The ice shell is ~20–25 km thick on average and thinner at the south pole — MODEL ESTIMATES — Čadek et al. 2016; Thomas et al. 2016.
- Salts (sodium salts) in plume/E-ring ice grains — MEASURED (Cassini Cosmic Dust Analyzer) — Postberg et al. 2009 & 2011, Nature.
- Organic compounds, including complex macromolecular organics — MEASURED (CDA + INMS) — Waite et al. 2009; Postberg et al. 2018, Nature 558.
- Molecular hydrogen (H₂) in the plume, consistent with ongoing hydrothermal water–rock reactions that could supply
  chemical energy usable by microbes — H₂ MEASURED (INMS); hydrothermal source and energy availability are INTERPRETATIONS
  — Waite et al. 2017, Science 356. (Silica nanograins also point to hot water–rock interaction: Hsu et al. 2015, Nature 519.)
- Phosphorus (as phosphates) found in E-ring ice grains originating from Enceladus — MEASURED in Cassini CDA data
  (analysis published 2023) — Postberg et al. 2023, Nature 618.
- Saturn's E ring is supplied by Enceladus's plumes — MEASURED/ESTABLISHED — Cassini; Spahn et al. 2006.
- No life has been detected on Enceladus — FACT. Enceladus is considered one of the most promising places to search for
  life — SCIENTIFIC ASSESSMENT (NASA).
- "Hidden from sunlight": the ocean lies under kilometres of ice, so no sunlight reaches it — follows from the ice thickness.
