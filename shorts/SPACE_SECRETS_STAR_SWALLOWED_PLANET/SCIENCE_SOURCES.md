## Claims
| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Event ZTF SLRN-2020, ~12,000 light-years away, toward Aquila | Confirmed (3, 4) |
| Optical brightening in 2020, lasting ~10 days; infrared glow over ~6 months ("for months") | Confirmed (3, 5) |
| 2023: first direct evidence of a star engulfing a planet; planet likely a gas giant (≲10 Jupiter masses; press: about Jupiter-sized) | Confirmed; size described as "most likely" / "roughly" (3, 4) |
| 2023 explanation: star at the start of its red-giant phase | Confirmed (4) |
| 2025 (Webb, NIRSpec + MIRI, ~830 days after peak): star too faint for a red giant; main-sequence, ~0.7 M☉ K-type ("slightly smaller and cooler than our Sun") | Confirmed (1, 2) |
| Likely mechanism: orbital decay ("probably" in the narration) | Confirmed as the favoured hypothesis (1, 2) |
| Grazed the outer atmosphere, then plunged in; gas blasted from the outer layers expanded, cooled and formed dust | Matches NASA's description (1) |
| Hot molecular gas incl. carbon monoxide close to the star; colder dust farther out | Confirmed (1, 2, 5) |
| "To Webb, a single point of light": the system is unresolved; results come from spectra | Correct (spectroscopy, not imaging) |
| No threat to Earth | Correct (12,000 ly away) |

## Not stated as fact
- **Planet size and orbital history** are inferred and phrased "most likely" / "probably".
- **All engulfment imagery** is labelled reconstruction or illustration. No NASA collision footage exists, and none is claimed.
- **The spectrum graphic is illustrative,** not the published data. The CO label marks the feature's existence, not its exact position.
- **The brief's date:** it gives April 10, 2025 for the Webb findings. Search records confirm "April 2025" (the JPL release and the ApJ/arXiv paper), so the video says only "2025".
