# Editorial QA — "A Star Swallowed a Planet — Then Webb Found a Twist"
This is an honest scene-by-scene review of the actual render. No virality score is claimed.

## Hook (three candidates)
- **A (chosen):** a Jupiter-like planet already touching the limb of a huge orange star; recognisable on frame 0.
- **B:** the planet enters small from the corner; slower read.
- **C:** a huge, stretched, overexposed planet; not recognisable as a planet.
- **A was strengthened:** contact glow from frame 0, faster sink, a heavier gas trail.
- **Hook narration score:** 64 WORKABLE. It can't reach 72, because the mandated opener fills the first six words.

## Retention beats (measured on the render)
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–2.5 s | plunge hook |
| 2.5–8 s | the Webb setup |
| 8–25 s | where, flare and name |
| **25.5 s** | **mid reveal:** "first direct evidence of a star swallowing a planet" |
| 34–43 s | the red-giant hypothesis |
| **43.8 s** | **"Webb found a twist"** |
| 47–63 s | prime of life, then orbital decay |
| **63–67 s** | **second payoff:** the plunge |

| 82–95 s | one point of light, spectrum, no threat |
| 95–104 s | the final "WRITTEN IN LIGHT" |

- **Shots:** 17, averaging ~6 s. A new visual development lands every 3–7 s inside the longer shots: live curves, label reveals, orbit laps, the star deflating.
- **Infographic share:** ~20% (the flare curves, the Sun-vs-star comparison, the spectrum graphic). Everything else is cinematic CGI.

## Science framing
- **Labels:** every engulfment visual is labelled reconstruction or illustration. The JWST model is "CGI reconstruction · not NASA imagery".
- **No fake telescope images:** Webb is described as seeing a single point of light, with all details coming from spectra.
- **Hedged inferences:** planet size and orbital history are worded as inferred ("most likely", "probably").
- **No threat:** stated explicitly, with a galaxy-scale visual.

## Weak points (honest)
1. **JWST model:** simplified (a flat sunshield); the weakest asset.
2. **Spectrum graphic:** uses a decorative colour bar, although infrared isn't visible light. It is labelled illustrative.
3. **Mid-video reveal:** the brief asked for ~25 s. The 2023 evidence lands at 25.5 s, but the bigger "twist" lands at 43.8 s, because the narration needs the 2023 setup first.
4. **Long single scenes:** the 2023-evidence shot (9 s) and the orbital-decay shot (9.7 s), each driven by label and camera changes.
5. **Narration:** no original narration or storyboard was attached to the brief, so this script was written by me (/yt-script). Swap in your original if you have it.
