DESCRIPTION
-----------
What was one day like for the very first members of our species?

Fardin has a watch that throws him back in time — as a ghost. Nobody can see him, hear him, or touch him. This time he set the dial further back than ever: 300,000 years, to a rocky hillside in what is now Morocco, where scientists found the oldest known Homo sapiens fossils.

For 24 hours he follows a small group of early humans: a gazelle hunt with stone-tipped spears, a raw marrow feast, a stone-tool trick so clever it lasted 200,000 years, hyenas circling the fire at night — and a final discovery that changes how you see your own species.

Every fact in this episode is based on published research. Where scientists still disagree, we tell you.
